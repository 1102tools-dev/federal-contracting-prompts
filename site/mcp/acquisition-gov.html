COMMON QUESTIONS

About the Acquisition.gov MCP server.

Is the Acquisition.gov MCP server free?
Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account, ads or tracking.
Is it an official government server?
No. 1102tools is independent and built by a federal contracting officer. No federal agency runs an official production MCP server for Acquisition.gov (checked October 29, 2026).
Do I need an API key?
Not when hosted. For the local version: no API key needed.
Which AI apps does it work with?
Claude and ChatGPT through their directories, Google Antigravity (Gemini) today using the hosted URL in mcp_config.json (no account or API key), plus any AI app that connects to a remote MCP server by URL. Submissions are pending for the Muse, Grok, and Gemini directories and the Antigravity Marketplace. The local version works with any MCP client, such as Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Codex, Google Antigravity, and Gemini CLI.
How current is the data?
A copy of Acquisition.gov's FAR Overhaul text and agency deviations, refreshed daily.
Can anyone see what I search?
No. Searches aren't logged or saved, and IP addresses aren't kept. When the hosted server calls a government site, the request comes from 1102tools, not from you. Your AI app handles the conversation under its own privacy policy.
Can I use it with CUI?
CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information) is a question for your AI app, not for this server. The Acquisition.gov server only looks up public government data and gets only the search your AI sends, never your chat or files. Whether CUI can go in your chat depends on your AI app and your agency's or company's rules, and it's the same for Local and Hosted.
Who builds it?
A federal contracting officer who has worked in the 1102 contracting series since 2012. Most people who use 1102tools are small businesses, capture and business development teams, proposal writers and consultants.
Where is the privacy notice?
See the Acquisition.gov server notice and the 1102tools privacy page.
